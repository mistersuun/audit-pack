# Revenue & Profit Insights Guide

**Sheraton Laval -- 252 Rooms**
For General Managers, Directors, and Controllers

---

## Executive Summary

The Intelligence d'affaires (CRM) module captures every dollar flowing through Sheraton Laval -- room revenue, F&B by outlet and category, ancillary income, taxes, card payments, labor costs, and cash reconciliation. It pulls this data from the nightly Rapport Journalier (RJ), which records 117 columns of financial data every day.

**What you can do with it:**

- Spot pricing opportunities before your competitors do (day-of-week ADR gaps, occupancy sweet spots)
- Catch cost overruns in real time (overtime spikes, labor-to-revenue drift)
- Compare actual performance against budget every month, department by department
- Track which F&B outlets are pulling their weight and which need attention
- Monitor cash handling integrity across night audit shifts

**How to access it:** Log in and navigate to **Intelligence d'affaires**. The system has seven tabs: Vue d'ensemble, Revenue Mgmt, F&B Intelligence, Main-d'oeuvre, Cash & Recon, Paiements, and P&L & Budget. Each tab responds to the period selector at the top (30 days, 3 months, 12 months, 5 years, or custom dates).

## 1. Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

These are the numbers that matter most. Know them, track them, act on them.

### Room Revenue KPIs

| KPI | What It Means | Current Benchmark | Formula |
|-----|--------------|-------------------|---------|
| **ADR** (Average Daily Rate) | Average price per sold room | ~$191.82 | Room Revenue / Rooms Sold |
| **RevPAR** (Revenue per Available Room) | Room revenue efficiency across all 252 rooms | ~$56.33 | Room Revenue / 252 |
| **Occupancy Rate** | Percentage of rooms sold | ~29.4% (74 rooms/day) | Rooms Sold / 252 |
| **TRevPAR** (Total Revenue per Available Room) | All revenue streams divided by capacity | Varies | Total Revenue / 252 |

**Why these matter together:** ADR alone is misleading. You can have a $250 ADR and 20% occupancy -- that is worse than $180 ADR at 50% occupancy. RevPAR captures both rate and occupancy in one number.

*Example from your data:* At 74 rooms sold at $191.82, daily room revenue is ~$14,195. If you could move occupancy from 74 to 90 rooms (36% occupancy) even at a discounted ADR of $175, daily room revenue jumps to $15,750 -- an extra $1,555/day or ~$46,650/month.

### F&B KPIs

| KPI | What It Means | How to Calculate |
|-----|--------------|-----------------|
| **F&B Revenue per Guest** | Spending per in-house guest | Total F&B Revenue / Number of Guests |
| **F&B Capture Rate** | How many guests eat at the hotel vs. going elsewhere | F&B Covers / Occupied Rooms |
| **Beverage Mix** | Percentage of F&B from drinks (higher margin) | Beverage Revenue / Total F&B Revenue |
| **Food Cost %** | Cost of food ingredients vs. food revenue | Food Cost / Food Revenue |

### Profitability KPIs

| KPI | What It Means | Target |
|-----|--------------|--------|
| **GOPPAR** (Gross Operating Profit per Available Room) | Operating profit efficiency | Higher is better; compare month-over-month |
| **Labor Cost %** | Payroll as a percentage of revenue | 30-35% for full-service hotels |
| **Revenue per Labor Hour** | Productivity measure | Track trend, flag declines |

## 2. Revenue Optimization Opportunities

### 2a. Room Revenue

**Day-of-Week ADR Analysis**

The Revenue Mgmt tab shows your ADR broken down by day of week. This is your pricing playbook.

*What to look for:*
- If Friday-Saturday ADR is $220 but Tuesday is $155, you have a 42% weekday gap. Consider corporate rate programs or group business to lift midweek.
- If Sunday ADR drops below $140, evaluate whether aggressive weekend-extending packages (stay Saturday, get Sunday at 50% off) would fill rooms profitably.

*Where to find it:* Revenue Mgmt tab > ADR by Day of Week chart.

**Occupancy vs. ADR Tradeoff**

The scatter plot on the Revenue Mgmt tab plots every day's occupancy against its ADR. This reveals your "pricing power curve."

*Key insight:* When occupancy is below 50% (126 rooms), discounting to fill rooms almost always wins because the marginal cost of an additional room is low (~$25-30 in variable cost). When occupancy exceeds 70% (176 rooms), hold rate -- you have leverage.

*Example:* On a slow Tuesday with 60 rooms on the books, accepting a $159 corporate rate for 20 more rooms adds $3,180 in room revenue plus potential F&B spend. The variable cost for those 20 rooms is roughly $600, yielding ~$2,580 in contribution.

**Complimentary Room Impact**

Complimentary rooms (column CN in the jour sheet) are tracked daily. Every comp room costs you the ADR in lost revenue.

*Example:* If you average 2 comp rooms per night at your $191.82 ADR, that is $383/day or ~$11,500/month in foregone revenue. The system tracks this -- review the trend monthly and ensure every comp has a documented business justification.

**Corporate Account (Ristourne) ROI**

Corporate accounts with negotiated rebates are tracked under the Ristournes column. Current accounts include CNAM, Club de Hockey, Cumberland, FCSSQ, Ice Breakers, JHD, Kaldec, Spartans, Maple Soft, Max Puissance, NB Elite, and others.

*The question to ask:* Does each corporate account generate enough room nights at a high enough rate to justify the rebate? If a corporate account books 200 room nights at $175 ADR with a 10% ristourne ($3,500 rebate), that account generated $35,000 in room revenue. Compare that to what those rooms would have earned at rack rate.

*Where to find it:* Ristournes (column BC) are tracked daily in the jour sheet.

**Package/Forfait Profitability**

The Diff. Forfait column (BF) shows the net adjustment from package pricing. A negative number means the package discounted more than the individual components -- that is not necessarily bad if it drove incremental occupancy, but track the trend.

### 2b. F&B Revenue

**Outlet Performance Comparison**

Your five F&B outlets perform very differently. The system tracks each one daily across five categories (Nourriture, Boisson, Bieres, Mineraux, Vins).

| Outlet | Daily Range | Monthly Potential | Strength |
|--------|------------|-------------------|----------|
| **Piazza** (restaurant/bar) | ~$12,940/day | High volume | Main revenue driver |
| **Banquet** | Variable, ~$909K MTD possible | Event-driven, high ceiling | Highest single-month potential |
| **Cafe Link / Pause Spesa** | Moderate | Breakfast/grab-and-go | Consistent base |
| **Marche La Spesa** | Lower volume | Niche | Budget: $32.5K/year |
| **Room Service** | Per-occupied-room metric | Service offering | Guest satisfaction driver |

*What to look for:* Compare outlet revenue trends on the F&B Intelligence tab. If Piazza revenue is flat while occupancy is rising, your capture rate is dropping -- guests are eating elsewhere.

*Where to find it:* F&B Intelligence tab > Outlet Trend chart (monthly by outlet).

**Beverage Category Mix Optimization**

Not all drinks are created equal. Margins vary significantly by category:

| Category | Typical Margin | Optimization Lever |
|----------|---------------|-------------------|
| **Vins** (Wine) | 65-75% | Highest margin -- push wine lists, sommelier training |
| **Boisson** (Spirits/Cocktails) | 75-85% | Premium cocktails and upselling |
| **Bieres** (Beer) | 55-65% | Good margin, high volume |
| **Mineraux** (Mineral water, soft drinks) | 70-80% | Low effort, consistent |

*Action:* If your beverage mix is 40% beer and only 15% wine, shifting even 5% of that mix toward wine could meaningfully improve margins. The F&B Intelligence tab shows food vs. beverage split monthly -- watch for the beverage percentage to trend upward.

**F&B Capture Rate**

This is one of the most telling metrics: how much F&B revenue do you generate per occupied room?

*Formula:* Total F&B Revenue / Occupied Rooms

*Example:* If daily F&B is $15,000 across all outlets and you sold 74 rooms, your capture rate is $202.70 per occupied room. The F&B Intelligence tab tracks F&B per client over time. A declining trend means guests are spending less at your outlets or eating off-site more often.

**HP Deductions Impact**

HP (Hotel Promo) deductions (column BR) represent comped meals and promotional credits. These directly reduce your net F&B revenue. The system tracks HP Admin (BQ) and Hotel Promo (BR) daily.

*What to watch:* If HP deductions exceed 2-3% of gross F&B revenue, investigate. Are managers over-comping? Is there a service recovery issue driving excessive comps?

**Banquet + Location Salle Correlation**

Banquet food/beverage revenue (budget: $750K nourr. + $245K boisson/year) and meeting room rental (Location Salle, budget: $150K/year, ~$117K MTD) often move together, but not always.

*Key question:* When Location Salle revenue is high but banquet F&B is low, it may mean clients are booking rooms for meetings without catering. Consider minimum F&B requirements or bundled packages for meeting room rentals.

### 2c. Ancillary Revenue

These smaller revenue streams add up. At 252 rooms, even modest per-room improvements compound.

**Internet & Sonifi Revenue**

Internet (column AW) and Sonifi/in-room entertainment (column AT) generate per-occupied-room revenue.

*Benchmark:* Track internet revenue per occupied room monthly. If it drops, check whether complimentary tiers are cannibalizing paid upgrades. Marriott Bonvoy elite members get free internet -- as loyalty enrollment grows, paid internet revenue will naturally decline. Offset this by ensuring premium/high-speed tiers are priced attractively.

**Massage Service Utilization**

Massage revenue (column BA) is tracked daily. Calculate utilization as revenue / (available appointment slots x average price). Low utilization suggests a marketing or awareness problem, not necessarily a demand problem.

**Laundry/Nettoyeur Revenue**

Valet/buanderie (column AO) and buanderette (column AR) serve different markets -- business travelers (valet) vs. extended-stay guests (self-service). Track per-occupied-room to spot trends.

**Vending & Minibar**

Machines a liqueur (column AP) is a low-effort revenue stream. If it is declining, check machine placement and product mix.

## 3. Cost Control Insights

### 3a. Labor Efficiency

Labor is your largest controllable cost. The system tracks 132 rows of payroll data across all departments.

**Revenue per Labor Hour by Department**

The Main-d'oeuvre tab shows revenue per labor hour -- the single best measure of whether you are staffed correctly.

*How to read it:* If your hotel generates $30,000 in total revenue on a day with 400 total labor hours, your revenue per labor hour is $75. If that number drops below $65, you are likely overstaffed relative to business volume.

*Department-level view:* The system breaks this down by Reception, Chambre, Piazza, Banquet, Cuisine, and all other departments.

**Overtime Alert: Flag When Above 5%**

Overtime hours (HRES SUP) are tracked for every department. The Main-d'oeuvre tab shows overtime as a percentage of total hours by department.

*Rule of thumb:* Overtime exceeding 5% of total department hours signals a scheduling problem or understaffing. At time-and-a-half, every overtime hour costs 50% more than a regular hour. For a department with 2,000 monthly hours, 5% overtime = 100 hours. If the regular rate is $18/hour, those 100 overtime hours cost $2,700 instead of $1,800 -- an overspend of $900/month or $10,800/year, from a single department.

*Where to find it:* Main-d'oeuvre tab > Overtime Trend chart and Department Summary table (overtime % column).

**Staffing vs. Occupancy Correlation**

The system correlates labor hours with occupancy. On low-occupancy days, housekeeping and front desk hours should flex down. If they don't, you are paying for idle labor.

*Key departments to watch:*
- **Chambre / Preposees / Equipieres:** Hours should track almost linearly with rooms sold
- **Reception / Portier:** Can flex with check-in/check-out volume
- **Cuisine / Piazza:** Should track with F&B covers, not room occupancy
- **Banquet:** Should spike only around events, not carry fixed staffing

**Budget Variance by Department**

The Main-d'oeuvre tab includes budget vs. actual for every department -- both cost and hours. Red flags: any department running more than 5% over budget for two consecutive months.

### 3b. Card Payment Costs

**AMEX Fee Impact**

The system tracks six payment categories: Visa, MasterCard, AMEX Elavon, AMEX Global, Debit, and Discover.

*Cost reality:*
- **AMEX:** Typically 2.5-3.5% merchant fee
- **Visa/MasterCard:** Typically 1.5-2.0%
- **Debit (Interac):** ~$0.05-0.10 per transaction (flat fee, not percentage)

*Example:* If your monthly card volume is $500,000 and AMEX represents 25% ($125,000), you pay roughly $3,750-$4,375 in AMEX fees. If that same volume were on Visa/MC, fees would be $1,875-$2,500. The difference: $1,250-$1,875/month.

*What to watch on the Paiements tab:* Track the AMEX share of total payments over time. You cannot refuse AMEX (brand standard), but you can promote debit at the front desk for incidentals.

**Card Mix Trends**

A shift toward debit transactions reduces your blended merchant fee rate. Monitor this monthly on the Paiements tab.

### 3c. Cash Control

**Receptionist Variance Patterns**

The Cash & Recon tab tracks surplus/deficit by auditor. The Diff. Caisse (column 2 in the jour sheet) and Surplus/Deficit (column CA) capture daily cash variances.

*Red flags:*
- Any single variance exceeding +/-$50
- The same receptionist showing negative variances more than 3 times in a month
- A pattern of small consistent negatives (e.g., -$5 to -$15 every shift) -- this suggests a procedural issue or worse

*Where to find it:* Cash & Recon tab > Auditor Statistics and Surplus/Deficit Trend.

**Deposit Accuracy**

The system tracks deposit variances between what should have been deposited and what actually cleared. The Cash & Recon tab shows Quasimodo variance trends (the automated balance-checking system).

*What to do:* Review any week where the average variance exceeds +/-$25. Investigate individual days with variances above $100.

## 4. Cross-Reference Analysis

The most powerful insights come from combining data across tabs. Here are the analyses that drive real profit improvement.

### Occupancy Rate vs. F&B per Guest

*Question:* When occupancy rises, does F&B spending per guest increase, decrease, or stay flat?

- If it **increases**: Higher occupancy brings higher-spending guests (group business with banquets, for example). Good sign.
- If it **decreases**: You are filling rooms with discount-rate guests who eat off-property. Consider F&B packages or in-room dining promotions tied to discounted rates.
- If it **stays flat**: Your F&B operation captures a consistent share regardless of guest mix. Stable, but look for upsell opportunities.

*Where to find it:* Compare Revenue Mgmt tab (occupancy trend) with F&B Intelligence tab (F&B per client trend) for the same period.

### Budget vs. Actual by Department (Monthly)

Every month, review these budget lines:

| Budget Line | Annual Budget | Monthly Target | What Variance Tells You |
|-------------|--------------|----------------|------------------------|
| Chambres (Room Revenue) | $1,112,404 | ~$92,700 | Demand strength, pricing effectiveness |
| Piazza/Bar Nour | $370,000 | ~$30,833 | Restaurant performance |
| Banquet Nour | $750,000 | ~$62,500 | Event pipeline health |
| Boisson Piazza | $137,800 | ~$11,483 | Bar performance |
| Boisson Banquet | $245,000 | ~$20,417 | Event beverage capture |
| Location Salles | $150,000 | ~$12,500 | Meeting space utilization |
| Equipement | $120,000 | ~$10,000 | AV and equipment rental demand |
| Marche/Spesa | $32,500 | ~$2,708 | Grab-and-go/market performance |

*Where to find it:* P&L & Budget tab shows variance analysis. Revenue Mgmt tab shows room revenue budget vs. actual.

### Corporate Account Profitability

For each ristourne account (CNAM, Club de Hockey, Cumberland, FCSSQ, Ice Breakers, JHD, Kaldec, Spartans, Maple Soft, Max Puissance, NB Elite, etc.):

*Calculate:* (Room Nights x ADR Achieved) - Ristourne Paid = Net Room Revenue from Account

*Then ask:* Would those rooms have sold anyway at a higher rate? If the account books 50 rooms in your busiest month, the ristourne is costing you premium revenue. If it books 50 rooms in your slowest month, the ristourne is buying you incremental revenue that you would not otherwise have.

### Seasonal Patterns

Track month-over-month and year-over-year trends using the period selector:

- **January-March:** Typically softer. Focus on corporate midweek business and hockey/sports group blocks.
- **June-September:** Leisure demand peak. Push ADR, reduce discounting.
- **October-November:** Conference and event season. Banquet and Location Salle revenue should peak.
- **December:** Holiday parties drive banquet revenue; leisure occupancy varies.

*Where to find it:* Revenue Mgmt tab > ADR by Month chart and RevPAR Trend (set to 5 years for full seasonal view). Use the "vs A-1" toggle to overlay year-over-year comparison.

## 6. Monthly Review Checklist

Complete this review within the first 5 business days after month-end for maximum profit impact.

### Revenue Review (30 minutes)

- [ ] **Room revenue vs. budget:** Open Revenue Mgmt tab, check Budget vs. Actual. Is the variance positive or negative? By how much?
  - *Target:* Room revenue >= $92,700/month (annual budget $1,112,404 / 12)
  - *Action if below:* Review ADR by day of week -- where are you leaving rate on the table?
- [ ] **ADR trend:** Is ADR holding, rising, or eroding vs. last month and last year?
- [ ] **Occupancy trend:** Are you filling more rooms? Fewer? At what rate tradeoff?
- [ ] **RevPAR direction:** The combined picture. RevPAR up = good. RevPAR down = investigate whether ADR or occupancy (or both) is the driver.

### F&B Review (20 minutes)

- [ ] **Outlet revenue vs. budget:** Check each outlet against monthly targets.
  - Piazza Nour: ~$30,833/month target
  - Banquet Nour: ~$62,500/month target
  - Location Salles: ~$12,500/month target
- [ ] **F&B per guest trend:** On F&B Intelligence tab, is the spend per guest rising or falling?
- [ ] **Beverage mix:** Is wine/spirits share growing? That means better margins.
- [ ] **HP deductions:** Flag if Hotel Promo deductions exceeded 3% of gross F&B.

### Labor Review (20 minutes)

- [ ] **Labor cost % of revenue:** On Main-d'oeuvre tab, check the trend. Target: 30-35%.
  - *Action if above 35%:* Identify which departments are over and whether it is rate-driven (raises) or hours-driven (overtime/overstaffing).
- [ ] **Overtime by department:** Flag any department with overtime exceeding 5% of total hours.
- [ ] **Budget variance:** Review every department. Address any running 5%+ over budget.
- [ ] **Revenue per labor hour:** Is it improving or declining? Declining = you are adding hours faster than revenue is growing.

### Cash and Payments Review (15 minutes)

- [ ] **Cash variances:** On Cash & Recon tab, review any variance above +/-$50. Check for repeat offenders.
- [ ] **Deposit accuracy:** Review Quasimodo variance trend for the month.
- [ ] **Card mix:** On Paiements tab, note the AMEX share. If it is trending above 30%, consider front-desk training on encouraging debit for incidentals.

### Strategic Check (15 minutes)

- [ ] **Corporate accounts:** Review ristourne accounts. Any account that booked fewer than 10 room nights in the month -- is the negotiated rate still justified?
- [ ] **Comp rooms:** How many complimentary rooms were given out? At current ADR, what is the dollar impact?
  - *Quick math:* Comp rooms x $191.82 ADR = monthly revenue impact
- [ ] **Seasonal positioning:** Looking at next month's pace, are you priced correctly? Use the 5-year seasonal view to anticipate demand patterns.

## Appendix: Data Sources

All data in the Intelligence d'affaires module flows from the nightly Rapport Journalier (RJ), which captures:

- **117 columns** of daily financial data (revenue, taxes, payments, occupancy, cash)
- **132 rows** of labor data (hours, rates, costs by department)
- **Monthly budget** targets by revenue category and department

Data enters the system when the night auditor processes and uploads the RJ. Historical data can be bulk-imported from archived RJ files via the Import function on the Vue d'ensemble tab.

**Data freshness:** KPIs update after each night audit upload. Monthly aggregations recalculate automatically. Budget comparisons require that monthly budget targets have been entered in the Budget module.

**Questions or issues:** Contact the system administrator if data appears missing or inconsistent. The most common cause is a night where the RJ was not uploaded -- the system will show a gap in the trend charts for that date.
